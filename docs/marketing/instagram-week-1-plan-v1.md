# AGym Instagram — Week 1 Detailed Plan

Status: production plan, unpublished. Created 2026-08-05. Extends `instagram-editorial-plan-v1.md` §7 with a day-by-day shot-list-level brief for the launch week.

Founder constraints for this week, as stated 2026-08-05:

- **The AGym page is faceless.** No founder on camera. A separate personal athlete profile is under consideration but is out of scope here (see §8).
- **Discreet gym filming is possible**, plus some incidental footage.
- **Target: at least one reel per day**, weighted toward the app and gym-community themes.

Seven reels a week is roughly triple the floor cadence in the editorial plan. It's achievable in week 1 because everything gets filmed in two sessions before launch day — but read §7 before committing to it as a permanent rhythm.

## 1. Filming rules — read before shooting anything

**Never film an identifiable third party.** No faces, no tattoos, no recognisable people in the background, however incidental. Discreet filming in a commercial gym means your own hands, your own bar, your own phone, tight crops, and empty frames. If a person walks into shot, the clip is unusable — reshoot rather than blur. This is both a legal exposure and exactly the kind of thing that ends a channel.

**Own footage only.** Do not use stock clips. The brand system prohibits staged stock realism, and stock gym footage is instantly recognisable to this audience. "Random footage" is fine if it's yours: a plate rack, chalk dust, a rack pin, a phone screen, an empty platform, a bar rolling. Ordinary and real beats polished.

**Treat the footage.** Per Concept B: grain, hard crop, high contrast, and — where it fits — a duotone push toward charcoal/cream/orange. Untreated phone footage looks like everyone else's. Treated footage looks authored.

**No physiques.** No mirror shots, no body in frame beyond hands and forearms, no before/after, no exertion-as-spectacle. This is a rule from the brand system and it also happens to be what separates the page from every other fitness account.

## 2. Pre-launch — the two production sessions

Do both before Day 1. Total ~2.5 hours.

**Session A — gym filming (~60 min, one training session).** Film these clips, 8–15 seconds each, more takes than you need:

| Clip | What to shoot |
|---|---|
| G1 | Hands typing a messy note into the phone's notes app, close crop, phone slightly angled, gym floor visible but unfocused |
| G2 | A hand writing numbers on the back of the other hand, or on a crumpled scrap |
| G3 | Standing at the rack, hand resting on the bar, still — the "what did I do last week" pause |
| G4 | Loading plates, tight crop on hands and the plate edge |
| G5 | A phone propped against a water bottle showing the app, screen not readable at this distance |
| G6 | Chalk dust, a rack pin sliding, a bar rolling on the floor — texture b-roll, 4–5 short clips |
| G7 | The walk-out: empty rack, gym floor, low angle |

**Session B — screen recording (~45 min, at home).** Record at 60fps, full app, no notifications on screen, airplane mode:

| Clip | What to record |
|---|---|
| S1 | Typing a genuinely messy log, parsing it, correcting one wrong value, confirming |
| S2 | The uncertainty flag appearing on an ambiguous value |
| S3 | Copying the context block and pasting it into Claude or ChatGPT — the full answer, unedited |
| S4 | The history / personal-records screen, scrolling |
| S5 | Export, then delete-all, in real time including the typed confirmation |
| S6 | Accepting a plan and marking it done |

**Session C — grid assets (~30 min).** Run briefs A1, B1, A3 and carousel C1 through `social-design-prompt-v1.md`. Export at exact pixel sizes.

## 3. Launch day setup — before the first post

1. Profile complete per `instagram-editorial-plan-v1.md` §9: name field, bio, link to `/coaches`, avatar (brief D1), five empty highlights.
2. **Post the three posters within one hour, in reverse order of how you want them to read.** Instagram fills the grid top-left with the most recent, so post A3 first, then B1, then A1. A1 (the pain) ends up top-left, which is what a first-time visitor sees.
3. **Turn off "also share to feed" on every reel.** You said you're fine with reels not living on the page — this is how. The grid stays posters and carousels only; reels do reach work from the Reels tab. It keeps the profile looking authored rather than like a camera roll.
4. Then start the reel cadence.

## 4. The seven reels

Each is 15–35 seconds, 1080×1920, captions burned in, fully readable with sound off. Audio: use a quiet trending sound at low volume under the cut, or original audio with ambient gym noise. No voiceover this week — that's a face-adjacent format and you can add it later.

Every reel ends on an ask. A reel without an ask is a reel that starts no conversation.

### Day 1 — R-W1-01 · "The note"
**Pillar P1 · Track A, bridges to B · community + app · ~22s**

The strongest opener because every single person who trains has this note, and it *is* the product's problem statement.

**Hook (0.0–1.5s):** clip G1, hands typing. Text on screen, large: `every lifter has this note.`

**Beats:**

| Time | Visual | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–1.5s | G1, hands typing into notes app | `every lifter has this note.` |
| 1.5–5s | Push in on the note itself — genuinely bad, real | *(the note is the visual: "legs. felt weak. skipped last set. back tight-ish")* |
| 5–9s | Hold. Slight zoom. | `six months of these.` / `none of it useful.` |
| 9–16s | Cut to S1 — the same note pasted, parsed, one value corrected, confirmed | `so I built the thing that reads them.` |
| 16–22s | S4, history scrolling | `RAW > REVIEWED > READY` then `what's in your note?` |

**Caption:** "I have four years of notes like this. 'legs, felt weak, skipped last set.' Useless to me, and completely useless to any AI I paste them into — it has no idea what I did last week, so every conversation starts from zero. So I've been building the thing that reads the note, asks about the bits it isn't sure of, and keeps what I confirm. Post your worst one, I'll go first."

**Alt text:** Close crop of hands typing a short, messy training note into a phone, then the same note appearing as structured, confirmed training data.

**Hashtags:** #strengthtraining #traininglog #liftingcommunity #quantifiedself #buildinpublic

**Ask:** worst note in the comments. This is your highest-probability first conversation.

### Day 2 — R-W1-02 · "The loop, uncut"
**Pillar P3 · Track A · app · ~30s · FLAGSHIP**

The most convincing thing you own. One take, no cuts, no speed-ramps. The credibility is entirely in it being unedited.

**Hook (0.0–1.5s):** S3, the AI's answer already half-typed. Text: `watch it remember my last session.`

**Beats:** a single continuous screen recording — type the messy log → parse → correct one wrong number → confirm → copy context → switch app → paste → the model answers referencing your actual last session and the set you skipped. Burned-in caption at the moment the answer lands: `it knows I skipped the last set. I never told it.`

Close on: `no plugin. no integration. just the context, kept.` then `RAW > REVIEWED > READY`.

**Rules:** do not speed up the parse. Do not cut the pause while the model thinks — the wait is what proves it's real. If it takes 34 seconds, post 34 seconds.

**Caption:** "One take. Messy log in, corrected, confirmed, and handed to Claude as context. The part that matters is at 0:24 — it references the set I skipped, because I told the app, not the chat. Every AI conversation about training starts from nothing unless you paste your whole history in every time. This is me not doing that anymore. Early build, still rough in places."

**Alt text:** An unedited screen recording of a messy training note being parsed, corrected, confirmed, and then used as context in an AI chat that references the previous session.

**Hashtags:** #ai #claudeai #traininglog #buildinpublic #quantifiedself

**Ask:** "what would you want it to remember?"

| Time | Visual | On-screen text |

**Pillar P1/P5 · Track A · community · ~16s**

| Time | Visual | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|

## 7. Honest note on the seven-a-day cadence

Week 1 works because everything is filmed before launch. Week 2 has no such buffer.

Before committing to daily reels as a rhythm, watch two things across this week: whether reels 3, 5, and 7 clearly underperform 1 and 2 (a sign you're diluting rather than compounding), and whether replying to comments is starting to slip. **Replying matters more than posting.** A reel that gets four comments you answer properly beats two reels you ignore, because the comment thread is where a coach becomes a conversation.

If daily is sustainable after two weeks, keep it. If not, the floor of 2 reels + 1 grid post from the editorial plan is not a failure — it's the level that survives month three.

## 8. On the separate athlete profile

Worth doing, and worth keeping deliberately separate.

The AGym page can't carry a face, which caps how far the community content goes — community content is ultimately parasocial, and a faceless brand account has a low ceiling on it. A personal athlete profile solves that without compromising the AGym page: you train, you post as yourself, you appear in coach comment sections as a person rather than a logo, and coaches reply to people far more readily than to brands.

Two rules if you do it. Keep AGym mentions incidental on the personal account — one in ten posts, at most, and never as a pitch; the moment it reads as a funnel it stops working. And don't launch both in the same week. Get AGym's grid standing first, then start the personal account once this cadence is proven.

Worth a separate plan if you decide to go ahead — say the word.

## 9. Guardrails check for this week

Every reel and caption above was written against the claims rules in `instagram-editorial-plan-v1.md` §11. Re-verify before posting:

- No identifiable third party in any frame. No physiques, no mirror shots, no before/after.
- No medical, injury, nutrition, or prescription framing. "Back tight" appears as a logged self-report in R-W1-04 and is never interpreted, advised on, or resolved — keep it that way.
- No time-saved, adherence, outcome, or revenue claim anywhere.
- R-W1-05 carries `Early concept — not a live product` and contains no link, no demo, and no pitch.
- No invented client data. No fabricated coach quotes. The Day 5 caption says "I'm asking" — do not upgrade it to "coaches tell me" until the ledger actually supports it.
- R-W1-02 and R-W1-06 show only shipped behaviour. If any step doesn't work on the day, cut the reel rather than the truth.
