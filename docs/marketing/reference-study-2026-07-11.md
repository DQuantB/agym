## What the founder is selecting for

1. **Poster first, ad second.** A post should read as a strong editorial object in-feed, not as a SaaS explainer with a photo behind it.
2. **Typography is the primary visual event.** Oversized type can crop beyond the frame, interrupt an image, repeat, overlap, or carry the rhythm. It is not a caption placed over a stock image.
3. **Controlled collision.** Text, image, pattern, and crop deliberately overlap. The result is energetic and authored, not a clean card placed onto a background.
4. **A single charged color world.** Most references use two or three colors with deep contrast: red/black/cream, orange/ink/cream, or coral/deep teal. Color creates the atmosphere rather than acting as a small product-status accent.
5. **Editorial image transformation.** Source imagery is treated through duotone, grain, halftone, posterization, pixelation, hard crop, masking, or collage. It must not look like staged AI fitness stock.
6. **Athletic energy without literal gym-prop scenes.** Movement, pace, interruption, repetition, and physicality can be communicated through scale, crop, texture, and visual rhythm. A barbell/shoe/notebook still life is not the default visual language.
7. **Technical signal through systems, not dashboard cards.** Grid logic, coordinates, counters, repeated strings, registration marks, contour lines, or structured typography can indicate data/context without fake UI or pasted-on arrows.
8. **Slightly strange, cultural, and anti-corporate.** A familiar object can be transformed into an arresting graphic device. The work should feel made for internet-native athletes, not a safe enterprise fitness tool.

## Transferable visual grammar for AGym

- Use the product mechanism as a repeating editorial phrase or transformation, e.g. `RAW > REVIEWED > READY`, rather than a floating “context card.”
- Build compositions around large, intentional words such as `CONTEXT`, `REALITY`, `LOG IT`, or `AFTER`, then support them with small factual copy.
- Favor one surreal/abstracted training-adjacent object, a kinetic cropped detail, or typographic motion over a generated athlete portrait.
- Use designed distortion: stretched/cropped letters, repeat patterns, contour/wave fields, pixel translation, rough grain, and offset print registration—sparingly, with hierarchy intact.
- Reserve clean sans-serif typography for product clarification and required CTA/disclosure copy; expressive display typography carries emotional energy.
- Make every visual system test legible at mobile size: one large idea, one focal object or type gesture, and no dense fake interface.

## Explicitly not transferable

- Nike, NRC, or any other third-party logo, name, campaign phrase, proprietary layout, photograph, illustration, or distinctive brand identifier.
- The references’ heart/anatomical artwork; AGym must avoid medical/biometric imagery.
- Illegible decorative microcopy when it would be interpreted as product information.
- Aesthetic imitation of any single reference. The output must synthesize broad principles into original AGym work.

## Proposed AGym visual territories for concept-board review

These are alternatives for an internal concept board only. No full ads, publishing, spend, email collection, or performance claims are authorized.

### A. Kinetic type / data rhythm

Large cropped expressive `CONTEXT` or `REALITY` typography; a restrained numeric/repetition system translates a raw log into confirmed context. Palette: ink + acid orange/red + bone. Best for direct pain-point messages.

### B. Distorted-object translation

One original, non-branded training-adjacent object or abstract gesture transformed by pixelation/posterization into a clean structured counterpart. Palette: bone + vermilion + dark charcoal. Best for `messy log > confirmed context` without a dashboard.

### C. Motion-field editorial

A silhouette/abstract movement crop against an original wave/contour/running-line field, with a type-first headline and tiny utility metadata. Palette: deep teal + coral + cream. Best for a more athletic, less product-literal social identity.
